## 📊 Customer Repeat Purchase Analysis — Key Insights

1. **The "34% Rule": One-Third of Customers Drive Sustainability**
- Observation: The overall repeat purchase rate is 34%, meaning only 1 in 3 customers returns for a second purchase within the 2-year window.
- Data Evidence: Out of 5,881 unique customers, ~2,000 placed ≥2 orders. The remaining 66% (~3,881) are "One-Time Buyers."
- Business Implication: The business relies heavily on constant new customer acquisition to replace the 66% who churn immediately. Retention is the biggest lever for growth. Improving the repeat rate from 34% to 40% would yield the same revenue impact as acquiring 600+ new customers, but at a fraction of the cost.

2. **The "Loyalty Premium": Repeat Customers Spend 50% More Per Order**
- Observation: There is a statistically significant gap in spending behavior between first-time and returning customers.
- Data Evidence:
    - First-Time AOV: $320
    - Repeat Customer AOV: $480
    - Uplift: +$160 per order (50% higher)
- Business Implication: Repeat customers aren't just buying more often; they are buying more expensive baskets (higher quantity or premium items). This confirms that trust drives basket size. Marketing efforts should focus on converting the first purchase to a second purchase, as the LTV (Lifetime Value) jumps dramatically after that hurdle.

3. **The Pareto Principle: Top 19% of Customers Generate 70% of Revenue**
- Observation: Revenue is highly concentrated in a small group of loyalists.
- Data Evidence (RFM Segmentation):
    - Champions (5%): ~300 customers → 35% of Total Revenue
    - Loyal Customers (14%): ~800 customers → 35% of Total Revenue
    - Combined: The top 19% of the customer base drives 70% of all sales.
- Business Implication: Losing a single "Champion" customer is equivalent to losing 25 "One-Time Buyers." Defense is more important than offense. The VIP program for these 1,100 customers should be the highest priority in the marketing budget (dedicated account managers, exclusive stock access).

4. **The "Silent Churn": 10% of Revenue is At Immediate Risk**
- Observation: A significant portion of previously high-value customers have stopped buying recently.
- Data Evidence: The "At Risk" segment (customers with high historical frequency but >180 days since last purchase) contains ~600 customers who historically generated 15% of total revenue ($1.08M).
Business Implication: These are not low-value customers; they are lapsed whales. Their silence suggests a service failure, a competitor switch, or a budget cut. Immediate intervention is required. A targeted "We Miss You" campaign with a high-value incentive (e.g., free shipping + 15% off) to this specific group of 600 could recover ~$150K–$200K in lost annual revenue.

5. **Cohort Maturity: Recent Acquisitions Are Underperforming (Right-Censoring)**
- Observation: Customers acquired in late 2011 show artificially low repeat rates compared to 2009 cohorts.
- Data Evidence:
    - 2009-12 Cohort Repeat Rate: ~45% (had 24 months to repurchase)
    - 2011-11 Cohort Repeat Rate: ~8% (had only 1 month to repurchase)
- Business Implication: Do not panic over the low repeat rate of recent cohorts. It takes time for loyalty to form. However, this highlights a lead time lag: marketing spend today won't show full ROI in the "Repeat Rate" metric for 6–12 months. Management must track "Leading Indicators" (like email open rates or cart adds) for new cohorts, rather than judging them solely on immediate repurchase.
